ART AND DEVELOPMENT TOOLKIT

How the look gets made

Chopper starts as a crisp 2D pixel game. Later looks can move into voxel 3D, then into low-poly 3D if that ever becomes the right call. The tools below are what that path actually uses.

PHASE 1: 2D PIXEL ART

Walk cycles, item icons, and tilesets need software that treats pixels as pixels and does not blur them.

Aseprite

The usual tool for 2D pixel art and animation. Timeline for frame by frame walk cycles, palette swapping, and sprite sheet export. Paid, or free if you compile it from source.

LibreSprite

A free, open source fork of an older Aseprite. The interface is older, but layers and animation are enough for the first pass.

PHASE 2: VOXEL 3D

If the game ever takes a blocky 3D pass, voxels are the short step from 2D sprites. No traditional mesh modeling required.

MagicaVoxel

Free voxel editor and renderer. Tree elementals can be built from blocks, colored with a pixel palette, and animated frame by frame.

Blockbench

Free low-poly and voxel modeler. Good for boxy characters, like a lumberjack, and for simple skeleton rigs.

PHASE 3: LOW-POLY 3D

A later look could use simple 3D models with low resolution pixel textures and stronger lighting. That needs a full 3D suite.

Blender

Free, open source 3D suite. Model a low-poly lumberjack and trees, unwrap them, and animate them on armatures. Paint the pixel bark and leaves in Aseprite, then map those textures back onto the models.

ASSET NOTES

Custom art gets mixed with modified free assets. Itch.io and OpenGameArt.org are the usual sources. Import a sheet or a model, paint over the colors, and force everything onto one palette so the game still looks like one world.
